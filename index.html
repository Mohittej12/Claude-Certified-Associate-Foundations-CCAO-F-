How to use these simulators

  • Each exam has 60 items (multiple-choice and multi-response) mirroring the real CCAO-F blueprint weighting exactly.
  • Multi-response items say how many answers to select — pick exactly that many.
  • Click Start Exam on any exam tab to begin its 120-minute timer. Progress and answers are saved automatically in this browser, so you can leave and come back.
  • Use the question palette on the left to jump around and see which items you've answered.
  • Submit anytime, or let the timer auto-submit at 0:00. After submitting you'll see a scaled score, pass/fail, a domain-by-domain breakdown, and the rationale for every item.
  • Use Retake Exam to clear an exam's answers and try it again from scratch.
Scoring model used here: scaled score = 100 + (correct ÷ 60) × 900, rounded — the same 100–1000 scale as the real exam. A scaled score of 720 or higher is a pass. This is an approximation for practice purposes; the real exam's cut score comes from a formal standard-setting study.

CCAO-F · aligned to Exam Guide v1.0 (effective July 2026) — from the official study guide's logistics section.

Format & scoring

Items
60 scored items, multiple-choice and multiple-response. Each item states how many answers to select.
Time
120 minutes (~2 min/item). No penalty for guessing — never leave a blank.
Scoring model
Criterion-referenced: measured against a fixed standard, not curved against other candidates.
Passing score
720 on a 100–1000 scale. Cut score set by a formal standard-setting study of the MQC (minimally qualified candidate).
Result
Pass/fail + scaled score, plus percent-correct by domain. Domain percentages are informational only — the pass/fail comes from the total scaled score.

Retake policy & recertification

Waiting periods
Grow after each fail: 14 days after the 1st, 30 after the 2nd, 90 after the 3rd.
Attempt cap
Max 4 attempts per exam in a rolling 12 months. Limits are per exam — failing one doesn't block a different one.
Validity
Valid 12 months. On-time renewal = free, non-proctored assessment.
Lapse
Let it lapse and you retake the full exam at full fee. Major content changes may also force a full retake.

The Claude model family

ModelProfileBest for
HaikuFastest, lowest cost, lightest reasoning.High-volume, straightforward tasks: short replies, classification, simple extraction.
SonnetBalanced speed / cost / capability.Everyday knowledge work: drafting, summarizing, most analysis.
OpusMost capable, highest cost/latency, deepest reasoning.Complex reasoning, nuanced analysis, hard multi-step problems.
Decision rule — model selection: match the model to the task's hardest requirement. Default to the cheapest/fastest model that still meets the quality bar; escalate to a more capable model only when reasoning depth demands it. "Always use the most capable model" and "always use the cheapest" are both wrong — the skill is matching.

Cross-cutting terms you must be able to define

Prompt — the instruction + context you give Claude.
Hallucination — fluent, confident output that is factually wrong or fabricated.
Context window — how much text Claude can consider at once.
Bias — systematically skewed or unfair output.
Task decomposition — splitting a big request into ordered sub-steps.
System / custom instructions — standing rules that shape every response.
Iteration — refining a prompt based on the output you got.
Human-in-the-loop — a person reviews/approves before action.

The five reflexes that pass this exam:
  1. Verify specific facts against an authoritative source before sending.
  2. Match the model/feature to the task's hardest requirement.
  3. Anonymize sensitive data and follow policy.
  4. Keep a human accountable for high-stakes output.
  5. When output is weak, diagnose and refine — don't give up or switch platforms.
